# ₹10 Cr investment decision

Computed in `notebooks/analysis.ipynb` §8–9. Outputs: `outputs/tableau/investment_options.csv` and `outputs/tableau/experiment_power.csv`.

## 1. Rules applied
1. **Observed values and planning assumptions are kept separate.** Benefits use only effects measured in this data (point estimate and 95% upper bound). Costs are external assumptions; each is labelled and sourced where possible.
2. **No manufactured lift.** v1 assumed a 3–6% uplift; v2 removes that.
3. **No extrapolation beyond the evidence.** Extra volume is capped at doubling today's volume. In 99% of account-months, an account receives at most 2 touches per channel. Per-touch effects measured in that range say nothing about 10× the volume.
4. **Break-even test.** To repay ₹10 Cr over 3 years, an option must add **₹3.33 Cr/yr ≈ 1.5% of the ₹217 Cr annual recovery**.

**How to read it**
- *Incremental recovery* and its *confidence range* are **observed** (effect per touch × extra volume). Everything involving cost is an **assumption**.
- ROI and break-even use the point estimate. **No option's effect is statistically significant**, so these columns show what the data's best guess implies; they are not forecasts.
- "Investment over 3 yrs" is the most each option can absorb at doubled volume. **None reaches ₹10 Cr except telephony**, which is the one option the data rejects. The budget is mis-sized for a 30,000-account book with ₹217 Cr/yr recovery.

## 5. Recommendation
1. **Do not commit the ₹10 Cr to any single option now.** The evidence supports none of them at that scale.
2. **Lead option: WhatsApp / digital engagement**, deployed inside a randomised test.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Spend | ≈₹0.2–0.5 Cr/yr running cost; ≈₹1.5 Cr maximum over 3 years |
| Expected incremental recovery (point, **not significant**) | +₹1.48 Cr/yr gross; +₹1.0–1.3 Cr/yr net of cost |
| 95% range of net benefit | −₹1.0 Cr/yr to +₹3.3 Cr/yr |
| ROI (point) | ≈ +200% to +600% on running cost; not statistically supported |
| Break-even | the benefit needs ≈₹0.2–0.5 Cr/yr, i.e. ≈0.1–0.2% of recovery |
| Downside | net ≈ −₹1.0 Cr/yr if the true effect sits at the low end of the CI; stoppable at any time |
| Confidence | **Low** on size; the direction is uncertain (CI includes zero) |

**Caveat — winner's curse:** WhatsApp is the largest of five non-significant channel estimates; with five channels, the largest point estimate is expected to be inflated by chance. This is why it is a *test arm*, not a rollout.

3. **Run a 6-month randomised test** (≈₹0.5–1 Cr; ASSUMPTION) on the whole book:

| Arm | Accounts | Treatment | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|---|
| A. BAU | 10,000 | current strategy, 08:00–19:00 rule enforced | control |
| B. WhatsApp-first | 10,000 | 2× WhatsApp, ½ dials | tests the lead option |
| C. Reduced contact | 10,000 | ½ of all BAU touches | tests whether current spend buys anything |

**Minimum detectable effect:** ≈6.6% of recovery per arm (₹398 per account-month). The test can catch **large** wins or harms; it **cannot** prove the small lift that would justify WhatsApp's running cost. This is a property of a small, noisy book, not a design flaw.

4. **Hold ≥ ₹8.5 Cr.** Release it only under a pre-registered rule: scale an arm when the 95% CI on ₹ per account excludes zero and the lift exceeds that arm's own break-even.
5. **Zero-cost actions now:**
    - enforce 08:00–19:00 IST calling (54% of calls are currently outside the window);
    - stop reporting month totals as growth;
    - verify PTP outcomes from cash.

## 6. What would change this recommendation
- **Finance unit costs** that differ materially from the assumptions above. The verdicts for agents, AI voice and field depend on them; telephony and WhatsApp do not.
- **A larger book** (e.g. extending the test to other portfolios), which shrinks the minimum detectable effect.
- **New data** (bureau, income, bank-statement) that gives targeting a signal to work with.
